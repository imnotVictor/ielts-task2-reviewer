# Case 03: Examples That Do Not Support the Claim

## Task 2 question

Advertising aimed at children should be banned. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

## Student essay

Children see advertisements on television, social media and websites every day. I largely agree that advertising directed at them should be prohibited because young people are easily influenced and their health may be harmed.

The strongest reason for a ban is that children cannot always recognise the commercial purpose of an advertisement. They may believe that a colourful video is simply entertainment and then repeatedly ask their parents to buy the product. This creates pressure inside families and teaches children to connect happiness with shopping. For example, a famous football player once appeared in an advertisement wearing an expensive watch. Many adults discussed the watch online, and the company received a great deal of attention. This shows that advertising is powerful and should not be shown to children.

Advertising can also encourage unhealthy habits. Food companies often use cartoon characters, competitions and gifts to make sugary products attractive. A child may choose a cereal because of the character on the box rather than its nutritional value. If this happens regularly, advertising could contribute to poor eating habits. For instance, some supermarkets place sweets close to the payment area, where customers can see them while waiting. This placement often makes adults add an extra snack to their basket.

However, a complete ban might be difficult to enforce online. A more practical policy would prohibit advertisements for products that can harm children and require clear labels on other commercial content.

In conclusion, advertising aimed at children needs strict control because children are vulnerable consumers and unhealthy products can damage them. Targeted restrictions would be more realistic than allowing companies to advertise without limits.
