# Scoring Basis and Limitations

Retrieved: 2026-07-29

## Official public sources consulted

1. IELTS, “IELTS Writing test preparation resources”  
   https://ielts.org/take-a-test/preparation-resources/writing-test-resources
2. IELTS, “IELTS General Training: Writing test format”  
   https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-general-training-test/ielts-general-training-format-writing
3. British Council, “Writing Band Descriptors”  
   https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/sites/default/files/ielts_writing_band_descriptors.pdf

These sources identify the four Writing Task 2 assessment criteria:

- Task Response;
- Coherence and Cohesion;
- Lexical Resource;
- Grammatical Range and Accuracy.

They also explain that Task 2 requires a connected essay of at least 250 words and that ideas should answer the prompt, be relevant, and be developed and supported.

## How this project uses the sources

The reviewer uses original instructions that paraphrase the public criteria. It does not reproduce proprietary teaching materials, commercial model answers, or full descriptor tables.

The four criteria are treated as equally weighted when calculating the displayed overall training reference score. The result is rounded to the nearest half band for study convenience.

## Limitations

The reviewer is an AI study tool. It:

- does not issue an official IELTS score;
- is not a certified examiner;
- cannot reliably detect every language or reasoning problem;
- may produce different estimates on repeated runs;
- must quote the submitted essay as evidence for its main judgments;
- should mark uncertain judgments as uncertain instead of inventing support.

Use the output as structured practice feedback. When an accurate score or high-stakes decision is required, compare the result with current official IELTS guidance or qualified human feedback.
